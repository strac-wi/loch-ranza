      Wheelhouse notes

      Handling a Voith Schneider double-ender

      Each unit is a rotor of vertical blades under the hull. Pitch, not rotor speed, sets the size and direction of thrust, so each unit can push in any direction through 360°. The pitch dot moves anywhere inside its circle. No.1 unit sits under the Gigha end and No.2 under the Kintyre end, both on the centreline.

      • Both units the same way moves her ahead, astern or sideways without turning.
      • Units pushing opposite ways athwartships turns her on the spot.
      • The pitch servos are slow, about 5 s from full ahead to full astern. Plan ahead.
      • Thrust rises with the square of rotor speed. On idle she makes about an eighth of full power, which is plenty for holding her on a slip.

      Simple controls

      Pick Simple on the start screen, or switch with Controls, under Settings, at any time. You only steer: W and S set the speed, and A, D, Q and E turn and crab sideways, springing back to centre when you let go. The crew runs the engines, puts the ramp down once you are square on the slip, works the traffic, holds her on, then raises and pins the ramp and tells you when you are clear to sail. Once the ramp is pinned and you are stopped, the crew also changes direction for you, so the end facing the next slip leads; to change direction yourself press C, tap Change dir on the touch overlay, or use No.1 leads / No.2 leads on the console or the wheelhouse desk. Landing too hard, grounding and leaving early still cost points.

      The RIB: Gala Viking V500 with a Suzuki 100

      Choose the RIB on the start screen. She lies alongside the Boathouse pontoon with the kill cord on. X starts the engine (lever in neutral), L lets go the lines or makes fast when you are alongside, stopped and parallel. W and S move the single lever: forward gear, neutral (it stops in the detent), astern, with throttle beyond each gear. A and D turn the wheel and it stays where you leave it; C centres it. R and F trim the engine out and in. K pulls the kill cord, O throws someone over the side, G picks a drill: coming alongside, man overboard or a high-speed slalom through six buoys in a line, weaving from west to east. On a phone, the thin slot on the left is the lever: drag it up for ahead or down for astern, it parks in the neutral notch on the way through and stays where you leave it. The long bar is the wheel and springs back to centre when you let go. Neutral snaps the lever to the notch and the Trim buttons work while held. New game takes you back to the start screen, which remembers your last choices.

      In a sea she pitches and slams: driving hard into a short chop costs speed and can launch her, so trim in and ease off, or take the waves at an angle. In the Sound of Gigha the ferry keeps crossing while you practise; she will not alter course for you, and if you are close under her bow she slows and gives five short blasts. At dusk and night she shows a white masthead light, red and green sidelights and a stern light; read which way she is heading from them. After a drill, Show track on the report opens the chart with your track coloured by speed and plays it back.

      How she handles: the engine steers by pointing its thrust, so with no drive there is little steering, and astern the stern goes where the leg points. At rest the bow blows off downwind. Trim in to get over the hump onto the plane, then trim out to lift the bow and pick up speed; too far out and she porpoises, and in a hard turn a trimmed-out leg blows out and loses grip. Full lock at speed scrubs off speed violently. Figures are estimates: 640 kg all up with two aboard, idling at about 2.5 kn in gear and topping out near 37 kn at about 5,900 rpm.

      Other traffic

      A yacht sails her way up and down, beating when the wind is ahead, and a fishing boat works her gear: a trawler towing in the Sound of Gigha, a creel boat hauling her strings in Loch Sween. Under sail or fishing they have right of way over the ferry and the RIB (Rule 18), so keep clear early and pass astern of them. They hold their course and speed, and act only at the last moment if you leave it too late, which costs points. Never cross close astern of the trawler: her warps run back a couple of hundred metres to the net. The creel boat counts as fishing only while she is hauling; running between strings she is an ordinary power-driven vessel and the crossing rules apply. By night they show their proper lights, and the chart shows them as targets with their heading. Lights quiz (on the start screen and under Settings) tests you on the lights and day shapes of these and other vessels.

      The tide

      The tide rises and falls with the clock: pick springs or neaps on the start screen. Under Settings you can move it to any point in the cycle, from six hours before high water to six after, change its size between neaps and springs, or hold it at one height to practise there. The rise and fall here is small, this coast being close to the point off Islay where the tide hardly rises at all; the ranges are estimates, not tide-table values.

      Pick Live on the start screen for today's real tide at the game's clock (GMT or BST), worked out on your device from the harmonic constants of the Port Ellen tide gauge on Islay: TICON-3 (Hart-Davis, Dettmering and Seitz, 2022, CC BY 4.0), from the GESLA-3 record. In the Sound of Gigha it is Port Ellen's tide, the nearest gauge on this side of Kintyre: the right size for the Sound, with Port Ellen's double high waters, though the times there may differ somewhat. Loch Sween has no gauge, so there it is the Tobermory gauge's tide shifted an hour and a half earlier and scaled down, the way tide tables work a minor port from a standard one, fitted to a week of published Castle Sween predictions (WillyWeather): its highs and lows came within about 5 minutes and 4 cm of them. With Live on, the tide controls under Settings stand down. Not for navigation.

      The chart

      Tap or click the corner chart to open it full size. Drag to move it, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use + and −; zoomed in, it shows the roads and the boats on their moorings. Follow boat keeps you in the middle again, and Close or Escape puts it away. Harbour and Route on the corner chart switch between close in and the whole passage.

      Loch Sween

      Pick Loch Sween under Water on the start screen (the page reloads; #sween in the address goes straight there). The RIB lies on a mooring in the arm below Achnamara. L slips the mooring; to pick it up again, come back slowly head to wind and stop with the buoy under the bow, then press L. There is no jetty in the arm, so the drills are picking up the mooring, man overboard and a slalom down the loch to the south-west. The shore, drying flats and depths come from charts: screenshots of a chart app round the moorings and down the east shore, and a photographed Admiralty chart for the rest of the loch and out past Danna, the MacCormaig Isles and Knap to Loch Caolisport; the roads and woods are traced from OpenStreetMap and the hills come from satellite height data. The flats at the head of the arm dry at low water and will put you aground. Castle Sween, the ruin on the shore near the mouth, is marked on the chart with a tower: one of the oldest stone castles in Scotland, its walls are 12th-century.

      Laying up at the South Pier

      When the cars are off at Gigha you are asked whether to lay her up for the night. Choose the South Pier and the waiting traffic is sent home; raise the ramp, pull away and follow the flashing yellow markers south down Gigha's east side. The last one brings you to the pier, where a green outline shows the berth along its fendered face. Come alongside parallel to the face and stop under 0.4 kn: the crew put the lines out and you get an end-of-day score. Let go to sail for Tayinloan again.

      Wheelhouse view

      Press V for the wheelhouse. You stand at the conning station of the leading end; changing which end leads walks you to the other station. On the main desk, from left to right: engine start, stop and clutch for each unit, the two throttles, the joystick (ahead, astern and sideways), the steering wheel, the two individual unit pitch sticks, then mode, conning-end and utility buttons. The amber dot beside each unit stick shows where the pitch actually is while the servo catches up. Look left for the deck console: hydraulics, and pins, Raise, Hold, Lower and Float for each ramp, with the deck display above it.

      Sailing from a slip

      1. Start both engines, keep them at idle and engage the clutches with pitch at zero.
      2. Hold her on with a little pitch toward the slip while the cars come off and on.
      3. Once the last car is off the ramp, start the HPU, raise the ramp fully and put the pins in.
      4. Set the conning direction so the far end leads, take the pitch off the slip, open up and go. There are no ropes at a slip: she is held there by power alone.

      Landing on a slip

      1. Line up on the slip about 300 m out and come in at walking pace, under 0.6 kn at contact.
      2. Keep gentle pitch toward the slip so she stays on it.
      3. Start the HPU, take the pins out and lower the ramp until it lands. Select Float so the ramp rides with the ship.
      4. Traffic moves only while she is held squarely on with the ramp landed.
