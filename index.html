      Wheelhouse notes

      Handling a Voith Schneider double-ender

      Each unit is a rotor of vertical blades under the hull. Pitch, not rotor speed, sets the size and direction of thrust, so each unit can push in any direction through 360°. The pitch dot moves anywhere inside its circle. No.1 unit sits under the Gigha end and No.2 under the Kintyre end, both on the centreline.

      • Both units the same way moves her ahead, astern or sideways without turning.
      • Units pushing opposite ways athwartships turns her on the spot.
      • The pitch servos are slow, about 5 s from full ahead to full astern. Plan ahead.
      • Thrust rises with the square of rotor speed. On idle she makes about an eighth of full power, which is plenty for holding her on a slip.

      Simple controls

      Pick Simple on the start screen, or switch with the Controls button at any time. You only steer: W and S set the speed, and A, D, Q and E turn and crab sideways, springing back to centre when you let go. The crew runs the engines, puts the ramp down once you are square on the slip, works the traffic, holds her on, then raises and pins the ramp and tells you when you are clear to sail. Once the ramp is pinned and you are stopped, the crew also changes direction for you, so the end facing the next slip leads; to change direction yourself press C, tap Change dir on the touch overlay, or use No.1 leads / No.2 leads on the console or the wheelhouse desk. Landing too hard, grounding and leaving early still cost points.

      Wheelhouse view

      Press V for the wheelhouse. You stand at the conning station of the leading end; changing which end leads walks you to the other station. On the main desk, from left to right: engine start, stop and clutch for each unit, the two throttles, the joystick (ahead, astern and sideways), the steering wheel, the two individual unit pitch sticks, then mode, conning-end and utility buttons. The amber dot beside each unit stick shows where the pitch actually is while the servo catches up. Look left for the deck console: hydraulics, mooring line, and pins, Raise, Hold, Lower and Float for each ramp, with the deck display above it.

      Sailing from a slip

      1. Start both engines, keep them at idle and engage the clutches with pitch at zero.
      2. Hold her on with a little pitch toward the slip while the cars come off and on.
      3. Once the last car is off the ramp, start the HPU, raise the ramp fully and put the pins in.
      4. Let go the line. Set the conning direction so the far end leads, open up and go.

      Landing on a slip

      1. Line up on the slip about 300 m out and come in at walking pace, under 0.6 kn at contact.
      2. Keep gentle pitch toward the slip so she stays on it.
      3. Start the HPU, take the pins out and lower the ramp until it lands. Select Float so the ramp rides with the ship.
      4. Traffic moves only while she is held squarely on with the ramp landed.
